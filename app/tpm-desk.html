The three sources of rules

      
None is sufficient alone, and that's the main lesson. The workbook declares its own rules but
        misses some; the field dictionary fills the gap; the identifier check digit is written down
        nowhere at all.

      

        
236rules in the workbook

        
80lengths in the dictionary

        
13digits the check digit covers

      


      
Writing traps

      
    
        
  • Never save the target workbook with a third-party library: the package gets rebuilt and the
          platform refuses it. Write straight into the XML.
    • 
        
  • Column letters in the export are not stable between files. Everything is located by technical
          path.
    • 
        
  • Identifiers are written as text, leading zeros kept.
    • 
        
  • Dates are written day/month/year.
    • 
        
  • A template with a single packaging level loses the levels above it. Say so.
    • 
        
  • An extraction is bound to the account it came from. With no partner identifier column,
          products land under that account.
    • 
      


      
Recurring source-data defects

      
Report these to the client; don't silently paper over them.

      
    
        
  • Brand owner missing, on 12% to 82% of products depending on the export.
    • 
        
  • Partner identifiers padded with zeros, whose check digit does not compute.
    • 
        
  • Labels reduced to the brand name alone, visible to the shopper.
    • 
        
  • Cases declared twice with different identifiers and identical quantities.
    •
